What changed from Rev A, and why it mattered Rev A drew the frosted rectangle as a through slot. It is not one. It is a piece of acrylic sitting on the main plate, making a step — so on this revision that whole rectangle is solid material standing 6.00 above the plate face, not a hole. Nothing sprays through it. The one opening in the part is the window below it, and you have set that to 98.00 wide, which pushed each side leg out from 17.10 to 26.00.

Worth saying plainly: I could not tell a step from a hole in photographs of a clear part, and I guessed the wrong one. The fix is cheap here because it happened on a sheet. Had it gone straight to an STL you would have printed a mask with a hole where the standoff belongs.

Open questions

  1. “An additional 6 mm” — from what datum? Drawn as 6.00 proud of the plate face, so 11.00 from the underside. The other reading is 6.00 more than the acrylic strip already there, which would make it taller by whatever that strip measures. If it is the second, caliper the existing strip and I will add to it.
  2. Caliper the step for me. Its 82.40 × 22.20 footprint is scaled off a photo against the 126.80, so ±1.5 mm. Every other figure on this sheet is either yours or off your calipers.
  3. Going 126.8 → 150, does the step stay centred? I have centred it, 33.80 each end. On the original it sat roughly 17 one side and 21 the other. Same question for its 2.40 margin from the front edge — that is a thin rim and I would rather know it than scale it.
  4. Does the window stay 27.00 deep? You gave me 98.00 across; the 27.00 is still the caliper reading off the original.
  5. The feet: 12.00 total, or 12.00 proud? Drawn as 12.00 overall, standing on the same face as the step. If they instead hang below to hook the far edge of the Studio Light part, that flips the print orientation and I need to know before the STL.
  6. What is the step for? If it is a standoff that holds the mask off the surface, its height is functional and wants a tolerance. If it just stiffens the plate, 6.00 is free to move.

Built at run time from the same constants as the drawings, with a real depth buffer, so the spray opening shows its 5.00 reveal instead of reading as foil. The orientation it opens in is the orientation it prints in — flat face on the glass, step and feet growing upward. Flip over shows the underside, which is the face that beds against the part.

The one thing to watch is the underside. That face is both the first layer and the face that beds against the Studio Light part, so elephant’s foot lands exactly where flatness matters. A 0.40 chamfer around the outline and the opening puts the squeeze outside the sealing face — say the word and I will draw it in. It is not on the sheet yet because it changes the 98.00 you just gave me by 0.40 at the very bottom edge.

PLA is fine unless the glue is hot. If the adhesive is a solvent cement rather than a spray contact adhesive, PLA will craze — PETG instead, same printer. Either way treat it as a consumable: cured adhesive bonds to both.

What happens next

  1. The STL is cut and audited — exports/spray-guide-revC.stl, 200 triangles, closed and orientable with zero unpaired edges, mesh volume agreeing with the analytic column sum to the last decimal. It was generated straight from the constants by scripts/spray-guide-stl.py, not modelled by hand in SketchUp.
  2. Slice it flat side down and print it. No supports, no brim.
  3. Questions 1, 2 and 5 are still open and the STL answers them by assumption, not by knowledge: the step is 6.00 proud of the plate face, its footprint is the photo-scaled 82.40 × 22.20, and the feet stand 12.00 total on the same face as the step. If any of those is wrong, the constants at the top of the script change and it re-cuts in a second.
  4. Print one, mask a real part, and whatever the spray pattern says beats every number on this sheet.
