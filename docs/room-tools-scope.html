What each one does

Effort is a rough sitting count. The hard parts are named because they are where these go wrong, and every one of them is something your own CLAUDE.md already warns about.

01

Auto-dimension

auto-dimension.rb ~2 sittings · BUILD FIRST

Select a room. It reads the interior floor face — the measured polygon is the truth, so everything derives from that — walks its outer loop, merges collinear edges into runs, and draws a chain dimension on every run plus an overall outside it. On all four sides, every notch and return, not just the bounding box.

  • windingWhich side is outside cannot be hard-coded. Two rooms in one script have already wound opposite ways and built walls inward. It computes the signed area and derives each run's outward normal from that.
  • closureSegments must sum to the overall, and it says so out loud. A chain that doesn't close means a wall face was misread. It prints the closure per axis and flags the failure rather than drawing a plausible wrong number.
  • doorsEach door dimensioned corner → near jamb, then the opening width. Read off the WR-Doors tag rather than guessed from wall gaps.
  • roundingNearest inch on the drawing, a tenth in the console table. Chain lines carry segment lengths only — never running totals, which is a real drafting error and reads as fact.
Produces dimension entities on WR-Dims + a per-run table with decimals + a closure statement
02

Room from a take-off

build-room.rb ~1.5 sittings after 01

The panel above, as an HtmlDialog. Type the runs the way you read them off a plan — a direction and a length each — and it previews the polygon live and tells you whether it closes before anything is built. Then: floor face, walls built outward from the interior face and mitred at the corners, 8'-0" ceiling labelled as the house default, doors as real openings with swings, standard materials, correct tags.

Then it calls 01 and the room comes out dimensioned. That is why 01 goes first — build it the other way round and you write the dimension logic twice.

  • closureA take-off that doesn't close is the normal case, not the exception — the dialog shows the gap in inches so you can find the misread wall on the plan instead of in the model.
  • mitresOuter corners come from intersecting adjacent offset edges. Extending each wall by its thickness at both ends makes them cross into an X at every corner.
Produces a built, dimensioned, tagged room from typed numbers in about a minute
05

Proposal scenes

proposal-scenes.rb ~1 sitting

Creates the five plates in order — 01-exterior, 02-dimensioned, 03-side, 04-ventilation, 05-plan — with cameras placed, and each scene storing its own tag visibility and style. That last part is the real work: dimensions on for plate 02 and off everywhere else, the plan parallel-projected with the door swing visible, the ventilation view aimed at the vented wall.

Then export-scenes.rb already turns them into correctly-named PNGs, and the filenames drop straight into proposal-v2.json. That closes the loop from model to proposal with no hand-naming.

  • tasteThe exact camera angle is your call, not mine. It places sensible defaults; you nudge and re-save the scene. The value here is the naming, the ordering and the per-scene tag state — not perfect framing.
Produces 5 named scenes → export-scenes → a proposal-ready plate set

Three more worth having

Picked because each one catches a mistake before a customer sees it, which is where the value is on this work.

Pre-flight audit

Read-only. Checks a model against your own standards before anything is exported: units Architectural, ceiling height labelled or flagged as the house default, all four sides dimensioned, every chain closing, tags correct, no stray geometry outside a group.

~1 sitting · your notes say a half-dimensioned drawing has already reached a client once

Delivery path check

Panel widths — 7 / 16 / 19 / 22 / 28 / 31 / 40 / 43 / 46″, plus the 49″ wide-access and ADA frame — against a corridor, doorway, elevator or stair you enter. Answers "can it physically get there" with the catalogue's own numbers rather than a guess.

~half a sitting · cheap to build, expensive to discover late

Fit and clearance check

Drop a booth into a room and have it draw and verify the real clearances: 1″ nominal, 6″ vented or 10″ with fan silencers, door swing by frame width, 45.625″ for an ADA ramp. Checks ceiling height first, because that disqualifies faster than floor area does.

~1.5 sittings · it puts the numbers on the drawing instead of in your head
