Section on the axis — every bore in one view

The part is one revolved profile, so a single section through the axis describes the whole of it. Two changes to read here: the tube guide opened 0.20 on diameter to ⌀10.10, and the housing socket cut to 6.50 so that flange face to the housing/tube junction comes to your 10.00. Housing and tube are ghosted in position, which is what makes the point of the change visible — 9.00 of housing now hangs below the flange, where your fingers can reach it.

What the socket depth is actually for An earlier pass at this revision grew the flange to 7.00 on the theory that the jig stands on a bench with the housing tucked up inside it. It does not. The housing is held in the fingers and the jig is pushed onto it, so every millimetre of flange is a millimetre of housing you cannot reach. The flange is back to 3.50, which is its floor — below that the underside is steeper than 45° and needs supports.

The governing dimension is now yours: flange face to the housing/tube junction = 10.00. That junction is the shoulder the housing butts against, so FLANGE_H + SOCKET_DEPTH = 10.00, and with the flange at its 3.50 floor the socket gets the remaining 6.50. Total height falls to 46.00, which is 11.50 off Rev A rather than the 5.00 first asked for — the 10.00 spec is the tighter constraint and it wins. 9.00 of housing now hangs below the flange for you to hold.

Cutaway — drag to orbit

The revolved solid, built live from the same constants. A quarter is cut away by default so the bores are visible; the cut faces are shaded, the outside surfaces are not. Print side up flips it to the orientation it has to print in — socket opening upward, so the shoulder the housing seats against prints on top of solid material instead of drooping over the socket.

What changed from Rev A, and on what evidence

Rev A was printed and test-fitted. Both changes come from that fit, which makes them better sourced than the measured dimensions — a fit test includes this printer’s real behaviour, and a caliper on the mating part does not.

The two clearances are now separate constants. Rev A used one figure for both bores, so opening the tube guide would have loosened the housing socket you said fits well. CLEARANCE stays at 0.25 for the socket; TUBE_CLEAR is 0.45 for the guide.

What the short socket costs

The fixture holds two things straight, and it now holds them to very different standards. The tube is gripped over 36.00 of guide; the housing is gripped over 6.50 of socket. The housing is now what limits how square the joint comes out, by a factor of three — and that is a direct consequence of the 10.00, not something that can be tuned away.

But it is worth knowing which number is now the weak one: if a cured joint comes out visibly off, look at the housing sitting crooked in a 6.50 socket before you look at the tube.

Printing — unchanged, and easier

Socket opening up, flipped from how it is drawn. That puts the shoulder the housing registers against on top of solid material, so it prints as a clean flat layer instead of drooping as an unsupported ceiling over the socket. It is the one surface whose flatness decides whether the housing sits square.

Shortening it helped the print. At 46.00 on the same 21.49 bed ring it is noticeably squatter than Rev A, so it is less inclined to be knocked off the bed. Every sloped face is still 45°, so no supports. The corner relief at the seat is untouched and still pushes the internal corner 0.925 clear of the housing OD, so the fillet a nozzle always leaves cannot hold the housing off its seat.
