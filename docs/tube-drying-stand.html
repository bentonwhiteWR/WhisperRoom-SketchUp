Tray fit — why two stands and not one

Sixty pockets at five per row is twelve rows, and twelve rows measure 147.80 against a tray that is 135 long. It does not go in. Cut the same sixty into two stands of six rows and turn them ninety degrees, and the pair measures 126.50 across the tray’s long axis with 4.25 clear at each end and 8.55 at each side. Same tube count, and two smaller parts print faster and lift out one at a time.

Three-quarter view — drag to orbit

The same geometry the Ruby script builds, drawn live from the constants at the top of it. Each diamond is a real opening with its 2.00 wall thickness showing in the reveal, and the view is depth-buffered, so what sits behind a wall stays behind it. Drag to spin, or use the arrow keys. Print orientation flips the pair the way it has to go on the bed — wall tops down, feet last — which is the single thing about this part that is easiest to get wrong.

Plan — one stand, 5 × 6 on a 12.15 pitch

The grid closes exactly: five pockets and six walls come to 62.75 across, six pockets and seven walls to 74.90 deep. Each pocket floor is a 2.00 ledge frame leaving a 6.15 square opening — whose diagonal is 8.70, so a 9.65 tube cannot drop through it in any orientation, while the whole middle drains.

Wall elevation — the fence

One diamond per pocket, in every wall of both grids: 71 openings per stand. Diamonds rather than round holes because a diamond has no flank steeper than its own apex angle — these run 20.6° from vertical, where 45° is the limit. They cost nothing in accuracy: the solid 3.00 bands top and bottom still locate the tube over the full 25.

Section — the full width

A tube drops in until it lands on the ledge, catching it by 1.75 mm of overlap. Everything inboard of that is open, and the whole underside is clear at 4 mm on the two corner feet this cut passes through.

Printability — checked surface by surface

It must print inverted, wall tops on the bed. Right way up, the plate is an unsupported overhang held on four 4 mm pads. Inverted, every surface falls inside the published limits — and none of these figures moved when the stand got shorter.

Bed contact halved when the stand did — 1 609 mm² instead of 3 069, on a part that is still 62.75 wide and only 31.5 tall. It is not tippy, but the brim matters more than it did. Print the two stands side by side in one job and they brim together.

On the Dremel 3D45: 0.4 mm nozzle, so the line-width figures are exact. Set build plate adhesion to Brim — Dremel’s slicer defaults to a skirt, which does nothing for adhesion — and run the door open for PLA, since the enclosure cuts part cooling and cooling is what the 10.15 bridge depends on.

How straight a tube stands

The pocket is square and the tube round, so the worst case is the diagonal of the clearance square, wedged corner to corner. Lean is clearance over depth, which makes depth the lever — and depth did not change here, so neither did this. The diamonds do not enter into it: the solid bands top and bottom still span the full 25.

What changed from Rev B

  1. One 10 × 6 stand became two 5 × 6 stands. Five tubes per row, as asked, and turning the pair ninety degrees is what lets sixty pockets into a tray that a single sixty-pocket stand overshoots by 12.80.
  2. The script now refuses to build a set that will not fit. TRAY_W and TRAY_D are constants, and run aborts with the overage before it draws anything — a stand 12 mm too long is 50 g of filament and hours to discover.
  3. 98.6 g the pair, against 94 g for the old single. Four and a half grams more, spent on the extra set of perimeter walls. That is the cost of splitting it, and it buys two parts that print and handle independently.
  4. Nothing else moved. Pocket, ledge, diamond, foot and print-orientation geometry are byte-for-byte what Rev B had, so the whole printability audit carries over unchanged.
