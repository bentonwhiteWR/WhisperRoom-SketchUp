Wall elevation — the fence

One diamond per pocket, in every wall of both grids: 136 openings that take 18.5 cm³ of plastic out. Diamonds rather than round holes because a diamond has no flank steeper than its own apex angle — these run 20.6° from vertical, where 45° is the limit. They cost nothing in accuracy: the solid 3 mm bands top and bottom still locate the tube over the full 25 mm.

Plan — 10 × 6 on a 12.15 pitch

Drawn to scale. The grid closes exactly: ten pockets and eleven walls come to 123.50 across, six and seven to 74.90 deep. Each pocket floor is a 2.00 mm ledge frame leaving a 6.15 square opening — whose diagonal is 8.70, so a 9.65 tube cannot drop through it in any orientation, while the whole middle drains.

Section — near a corner

A tube drops in until it lands on the ledge, catching it by 1.75 mm of overlap. Everything inboard of that is open, and the whole underside is now clear at 4 mm.

Printability — checked surface by surface

It must print inverted, wall tops on the bed. That is not a preference: right way up, the plate is one 123 × 75 unsupported overhang held on four 4 mm pads. Inverted, every surface falls inside the published limits.

No enclosed voids anywhere, so there is nothing for support material to get trapped inside even if you leave supports switched on by mistake. The 10.15 mm bridge is the only figure sitting on its limit — raise LEDGE_W to shorten it if it comes out rough.

On the Dremel 3D45 specifically: 0.4 mm nozzle, so the line-width figures above are exact. Independent testing has it clearing 65° overhangs, well past the 45° baseline. Two things to set — build plate adhesion to Brim, because Dremel's slicer defaults to a skirt and a skirt does nothing for adhesion, and run the door open for PLA, since the enclosure cuts part cooling and cooling is what the 10.15 mm bridge depends on.

How straight a tube stands

The pocket is square and the tube round, so the worst case is the diagonal of the clearance square, wedged corner to corner. Lean is clearance over depth, which makes depth the lever. The diamonds do not enter into it — the solid bands top and bottom still span the full 25 mm.

What changed from Rev A

  1. Seven under-ribs → four corner pads. The ribs only existed to stop the plate being an overhang; printing inverted removes that problem entirely, so they went. The whole underside is now open to drain.
  2. 136 diamond openings. Took 18.5 cm³ out of the walls and gave the curing tubes some airflow.
  3. Round drain holes → square ledge openings. A 6.15 square drains far better than a ⌀5 hole and, printed inverted, leaves a clean 2 mm step instead of a hole closing in over a void.
  4. 94 g instead of 129 g — 27% less filament, and the print is shorter with it.
