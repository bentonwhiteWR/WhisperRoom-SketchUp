Direction

Switch to Radial and drag the spread again. Parts drift diagonally, cross each other's paths, and the eye can no longer tell which wall came off which face. That is why Axis is the default: each part moves along one axis only — whichever it is already furthest along — so panels come straight off their walls and the ceiling straight up. It looks acceptable in a viewport and wrong on a printed page, which is the only place it matters.

How it decides

Three rules, all of them about being readable on paper rather than impressive on screen.

One axis per part

The part's offset from the assembly centre is measured, and the largest of its three components wins. A wall panel sitting 24″ out in X and 0″ in Y moves in X. Nothing travels diagonally.

mode: Axis · Radial · Vertical only

Outer parts travel further

Distance scales with how far the part already sits from the centre, so layers fan out instead of bunching. A floor under the multiplier stops a near-centre part ending up buried inside its neighbours.

0.35 + 0.65 × (distance ÷ furthest)

Home is recorded, not inferred

The first move writes each part's home position onto the part as an attribute. Every later explode is computed from home, so changing the spread never compounds — and Reset is exact rather than approximate.

saved with the model · survives reopening

Leaders are cleared, not stacked

Leader lines go on WR-Explode-Leaders and every run wipes the previous set first. Three explodes at three spreads leaves one set of leaders, not three.

own tag · switch off for a clean render

Using it

Select the group that contains the parts — it explodes the direct children, because top-level parts are what a manual shows and going deeper produces confetti.
