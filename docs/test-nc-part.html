What I derived, and what I assumed

The outline comes from the cutter-compensation side, not from the stock. The four perimeter passes run at X0, Y8.875, X−81.125 and Y49, all under G41. Working out which side of each line the tool sits on puts the finished material between X0 and X−81.125, and between Y8.875 and Y49. The strip below Y8.875 is offcut. That is where 81.125 × 40.125 comes from — derived, and it depends on the compensation reading being right.

Thickness is assumed, not stated. Nothing in the file declares stock. The through passes go to Z−0.78 and the drills to Z−0.80, which puts the table just below the tool and implies ¾ material. If the stock is something else, every depth in the model above is still correct but the slab is the wrong thickness.

The rabbet is the least certain part of the drawing. Three overlapping rectangular passes at Z−0.375 and Z−0.5 surround the opening. I have drawn them as a two-level recess, which is what a window stop or a glazing rebate would look like. The passes do not quite overlap edge to edge, so the real floor may have a narrow ridge left standing between them that the model does not show.

The 24 holes and the round bore are read directly from the G81 coordinates and the two circle passes, so those positions are as exact as the program is.

The animation runs the file's own feeds. Every cut is timed at the F word that governs it — F90 for a plunge, F330 and F400 for the profiles, F360 for the drill cycles — with rapids at a nominal 500 in/min. So the clock is a fair estimate of cycle time at 1×, not a fixed-rate flythrough. Two simplifications: the 0.025 in ramp-on steps at the head of each comped profile are folded into the move they lead into, and the corner arcs are rebuilt as true quarter arcs rather than replayed as the post's individual chords.

Before this gets cut

This drawing is what the program describes. It is not what the machine will necessarily produce: cutter compensation is left switched on across a rapid and a plunge at lines 119–122 and again at 160–163, which moves the tool about a radius off the programmed point before it enters the material. The two long edges are also taken all the way through before every interior feature is cut, so the panel is loose for the last third of the program unless it is held down by vacuum.
