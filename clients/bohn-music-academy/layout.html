Plan — to scale, every wall as measured

All six measured dimensions are drawn as given. They describe a room whose two long walls are not parallel: 346½ across the corner-room end against 336½ at the far end. The corner room is a small enclosed room, not a solid block. Door and swing are on the 195¼ side, and there is a column at the far corner of that same wall.

The room is out of square, and that is the finding Across the corner-room end the wall runs 195¼ + 151¼ = 346½. At the far end it is 336½. Those ten inches are not a measuring error — they are the room. The two long walls converge by about 1.0° over 47 ft.

The first version of this drawing assumed a rectangle and “corrected” the 195¼ down to 185¼ to make it close. The measurement was right and the assumption was wrong.

With all six figures drawn as measured, exactly one residual is left: the wall outside the corner room computes to 561¾ against the 563¼ written — 1½ in over 47 ft. That is ordinary tape error at that distance and needs no explaining away. Every other wall closes exactly.

Why it matters for a booth. A booth is square and the room is not. Set one along the long wall and the gap behind it tapers by up to 10 in end to end. That is easy to live with, but it has to be planned rather than discovered on site, and it decides which end you set out from.

What the room gives you

Large, open and tall. None of the usual constraints bite. Reported for planning — which model goes in is the quote’s call, not mine.

What the photos add that the sketch does not

  1. Open structure ceiling, painted black, with a skylight. White wall to roughly 12–14 ft, black deck above with exposed joists, hanging linear fixtures and a large ceiling fan. A booth sits well clear of all of it, but those hanging fixtures are the only things anywhere near booth height — locate them before choosing a spot.
  2. Two electrical panels on one long wall, roughly a third of the way along. Convenient for a feed, and a reason not to block that wall.
  3. Carpet tile over slab, flat and continuous. No thresholds or level changes visible in any photo.
  4. The room is in active use — grand piano, seating group, bookcase, music stands, a riser. Where the booth goes is as much about the room’s use as about fit.

What I need before this becomes a proposal

  1. The quote link — sales.whisperroom.com/q/W-…. The model comes off the quote and I draw what is on it. At 27 ft of ceiling and 1,400 sq ft essentially the whole catalogue fits, so drawing a small one would read as a recommendation.
  2. The column’s size and exactly where it sits. It is the only thing on the plan with no dimension at all, and it is on the same wall as the door.
  3. The roll-up door’s clear width and position, and what is on the far side of it.
  4. Which wall the Academy wants it on, and whether the piano stays. That decides door swing and vent routing more than the dimensions do.
